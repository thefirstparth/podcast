  - **Order of a match.** Minutes said within a segment must not go back ("just before the hour" after the 63rd minute); back to the writer. The prompt asks for the order it happened.

- **The 20 Oct audit, ten changes** (Parth, 20 Oct: "go ahead with all ten"). Each was checked on the real pack or the real audio of 17 to 20 Oct before it went in:
  1. **Rival stories.** A headline that names the rival before the subject is the rival's story ("Barcelona face ultimate test: PSG and Real Madrid await..."), unless it is the fixture itself ("Barcelona v Real Madrid"). The title never uses a headline naming a rival; on a day with no result it is the next die-hard fixture ("Real Madrid v RB Leipzig, late tomorrow night").
  2. **The show always opens.** With no result, the cold open is on the biggest die-hard story or the next die-hard fixture, then the hello with the show's name. A script that does not open that way goes back to the writer.
  3. **One word across a cut.** A line's last word heard only at the start of the next piece (or its first word only at the end of the one before) rejects the cut ("Ukraine", heard as "Crane", played after the other host's reply; "Tests" split in two).
  4. **Women by name, and stories about their subject.** The WTA's ranked players (ESPN, every run, the last list kept in ledger/squads.json) are women's sport words: every name in full, and the surnames of the top hundred that are nobody else's (not an ATP player's, not a common surname such as Fernandez). A story must name its subject in the opening of its text (the Svitolina and Monfils split came in under Djokovic because he reacted to it).
  5. **Title claims.** A sentence saying a followed player won a tournament the data knows (ESPN's calendar), when he has not won it, is cut ("Novak just won Basel": Basel runs 26 Oct to 2 Nov). A title from before ("in 2015", "twice") is not judged.
  6. **Names said as other names.** The listening check lines up each line with what was heard; where a name is replaced by another name from the script ("Jodar" said as "Nadal"), that line is voiced once more on its own, and the new piece is used only if it is heard right. On four days of audio it found that one swap and nothing else.
  7. **Old details in new stories.** The ledger now keeps what the hosts said (`spoken`), and a story loses any sentence that says again what one of the last three episodes said (word stems against three lines in a row that name the same people). On 20 Oct's news it cut the Curry ankle scare, Diomande's strike against Sevilla and Güler dropping deeper, and nothing new. A story with nothing new left is dropped.
  8. **Cut-off names.** Article texts end on a whole sentence ("missing Jimmy Butler III and Moses").
  9. **Fixtures.** The next four days in full (and a die-hard fixture always), anything later in one line; grouped by day and shared between the hosts. More than three lines in a row from one host goes back to the writer.
  10. **Dates are not facts said twice** ("27 October" against "22 Oct to 27 Nov").
